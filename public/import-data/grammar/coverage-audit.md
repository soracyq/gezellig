# Dutch A1/A2 grammar coverage and editorial audit

Reviewed: 9 September 2026. Scope: the user's supplied grammar-curriculum prompt, the existing 17-column template, and the application's current reading/import model.

Result: **32 A1 lessons + 40 A2 lessons = 72 lessons**. Every requested essential area is covered at the depth described below. This is a CEFR-aligned teaching sequence, not an official CEFR grammar syllabus or a claim that reading these lessons establishes CEFR proficiency. The Council of Europe's [CEFR descriptors](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search) describe communicative ability; the particular grammar sequence and level boundaries here are editorial decisions.

“Covered” means the stated beginner learning objective is taught in the lesson text, rule and examples. It does not mean every exception or advanced use of the topic is covered. “Partially covered” identifies deliberate recognition-only treatment; “Not covered” identifies material reserved for a later course. There are no unresolved gaps in the requested essential A1/A2 scope.

## Linguistic checks and references

Explanations and examples were newly authored rather than copied from a textbook. Review covered agreement, articles, spelling, verb positions, translation, practical meaning and the intended difficulty. Specific points were checked against the following reference material:

- [Onze Taal: adjective endings](https://onzetaal.nl/taalloket/buigings-e-algemene-regels) and [Team Taaladvies: adjective inflection](https://www.vlaanderen.be/team-taaladvies/taaladviezen/bijvoeglijke-naamwoorden-verbuigen-verbuiging-met-of-zonder-e): the indefinite singular het exception, material adjectives and restricted role expressions.
- [Taaladvies: personal object forms](https://taaladvies.net/hen-hun-of-ze-verwijzing-naar-personen/): the distinction between unstressed ze and formal hen/hun uses.
- [Onze Taal: final verb-group order](https://onzetaal.nl/taalloket/rode-groene-volgorde): both orders are accepted for the simple auxiliary/participle groups taught here.
- [Team Taaladvies: perfect auxiliaries](https://www.vlaanderen.be/team-taaladvies/taaladviezen/voltooide-deelwoorden-met-hebben-of-zijn): auxiliary selection depends on the verb and its meaning.
- [Onze Taal: possessive spelling](https://onzetaal.nl/taalloket/bezits-s-algemene-regels): Piets, Anna's, Max' and the unstressed-e pattern.
- [Team Taaladvies: hoeven/moeten](https://www.vlaanderen.be/team-taaladvies/taaladviezen/hoeven-moeten): lack of necessity versus negative obligation; the course uses a clear Netherlands-oriented beginner distinction.
- [NT2: De opmaat](https://www.nt2.nl/nl/examens/inburgeringsexamen-a2/alle-uitgaven/100-17044_De-opmaat-herziene-editie) and its [grammar appendix](https://www.nt2.nl/documenten/de_opmaat/grammatica_bijlage.pdf): contextual checks of beginner reference material, not an official endorsement or a copied lesson inventory.

## Limits of this audit

This is an AI editorial review, not an independent native-speaker or qualified NT2-teacher sign-off. Before publishing as a professionally reviewed course, a qualified reviewer should check every explanation/example pair, level assignment, naturalness, terminology, regional nuance and reading-time estimate, and pilot the sequence with learners. References settle particular grammar questions; they do not certify the whole dataset.

The files provide lessons for reading and completion. They contain no exercises or assessment evidence because the current import schema has no exercise fields. Reading them does not certify mastery or a CEFR level. The import checks and their exact scope are documented separately in `validation-report.json` and `README.md`.
